# Research agenda — known gaps and next investigations

1. Counterfactual Analysis

    What to find out: In which electoral contexts (e.g., high-visibility statewide races vs. ballot initiatives) does high campaign spend fail to deliver wins, and what neutralizes its impact?

    Method: Secondary literature review & electoral dataset analysis.

    Tool: Perplexity (for grounded historical sweep) + OpenSecrets database.

    What it would change: Prevents treating financial persuasion as an absolute law and identifies environmental conditions that weaken ad efficacy.

2. Player Dynamics & Ad Efficacy Mechanism

    What to find out: How do political media buyers and campaign strategists evaluate the ROI of ad spend against voter skepticism and fact-checkers?

    Method: Expert qualitative interviews (3–5 campaign consultants across parties).

    Tool: Go talk to people (field research).

    What it would change: Moves the mapping from "voters are easily duped" to understanding the exact psychological levers media buyers optimize for.

3. Tech Flux & Cost Scaling

    What to find out: How are generative AI and automated ad generation altering the marginal cost per persuaded voter for under-funded campaigns vs. deep-pocket PACs?

    Method: Deep research sweep on emerging political ad-tech tools and regulatory filings.

    Tool: NotebookLM (synthesizing ad-tech whitepapers, FEC disclosures, and academic papers).

    What it would change: Uncovers whether AI democratizes campaign outreach or exponentially widens the gap for legacy donors.
